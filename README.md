# The Most Expensive AI Mistake Happens Before Any Code Is Written

![The Most Expensive AI Mistake Happens Before Any Code Is Written](most-expensive-ai-mistake-before-coding.png)

Many AI projects do not fail because of weak models or poor engineering. They fail earlier, when a team picks the wrong problem to solve. This repository explains why that happens and how **AI Opportunity Discovery Services** help business leaders decide which AI ideas deserve investment before any budget is committed.

AI Opportunity Discovery Services start with the business, not the technology. They look at where time, cost or revenue are at stake, and which ideas are realistic given your data and systems. The goal is a short, ranked list of opportunities instead of a long list of experiments.

Whether discovery is run with a partner or in-house, the same principles apply. This guide walks through the questions, process and warning signs that separate a strong AI roadmap from an expensive guess, and shows where AI Opportunity Discovery Services fit in.

## The Core Idea

Building the wrong thing well is still a waste. Before any code is written, every AI idea should be able to answer three things:

1. **What problem does it solve, and for whom?**
2. **What measurable result will improve if it works?**
3. **Do we have the data, systems and ownership to deliver it?**

If an idea cannot answer these, it is not ready to build.

## Five Questions Before You Commit

| Question | What a good answer looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Is the problem specific? | A named process, team or customer pain point |
| Is the value measurable? | A clear metric, such as time saved, cost reduced or revenue gained |
| Is the data ready? | Reliable, accessible and relevant to the task |
| Does it fit current systems? | It connects to existing tools without a rebuild |
| Who owns the outcome? | One accountable business owner after launch |

## A Simple Discovery Process

1. **Collect ideas** from across teams, without filtering too early.
2. **Map each idea** to a business problem and a target metric.
3. **Check feasibility**: data quality, integrations, risk and effort.
4. **Score and rank** ideas by value against effort.
5. **Pilot one use case** with clear success criteria, then decide whether to scale.

## Warning Signs

- The project starts with a tool or model rather than a problem
- No one can say how success will be measured
- The required data is scattered, missing or unreliable
- No business owner is accountable for results
- Success criteria are chosen only after launch
